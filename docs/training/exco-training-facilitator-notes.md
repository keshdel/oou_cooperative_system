# Exco Training — Facilitator Notes

Teaching notes for the two half-day course in the [lesson plan](exco-training-lesson-plan.md). One section per part: what to say, what to click, what people get wrong, and what to do when the room goes quiet.

**How to teach this room**

- Officers come from all walks of life. Some read easily; some do not. Use short sentences and ordinary words, and never let a hard word pass without explaining it once.
- Talk about the society, not the software. Say "money we are keeping for members" before you ever say "account 2000".
- Say numbers out loud as well as showing them. ₦20,000 spoken is clearer than ₦20,000 on a slide behind somebody's head.
- Show it, then let them do it. Nobody learns a screen by watching another person use it.
- Never train on real records.
- When a question is really about our rules — should we charge that fee, should that officer approve that loan — write it on the board for the exco meeting and move on. Do not let the class become a bye-laws argument.
- Praise the first person who admits they are lost. That one sentence decides whether the quiet officers ask anything all day.

---

## Part 1 — Why we moved (30 min)

**Open with a question, not a slide.** Ask: *a member says their savings is wrong — how do we know who is right?* Let two or three officers answer. Their answers will describe three different records and a lot of trust. That is the problem. You do not need to explain it yourself.

**Three things to land**

1. **One place for everything.** We all work in the same system, and the accounts are written the same second. Nobody rebuilds anything at month end.
2. **Nothing is rubbed out.** You cannot delete. You cancel, and the system asks why. That protects the officer as much as the member.
3. **We decide who does what.** What a Treasurer can open is our decision, and we can change it any day.

**Open Settings → Task Assignment on the screen.** Show the duties grouped by area, and each officer's own name. Say plainly that the President holds everything and can never be locked out — somebody must always be able to open the door again after a mistake.

**Expect this question:** *can the Treasurer see every member?* Yes, unless we change it. An officer who cannot look a member up cannot answer that member.

---

## Part 2 — Logging in and looking around (20 min)

Everybody on their own laptop. Do not do this alone at the front.

**What they do:** log in → change the password → click your name → **Security & 2FA** → point the phone at the picture → type the six numbers.

**Say why in one sentence:** *your password is the key to the society's money, and this makes the key useless without your phone.*

Walk around and help. The officer who skips this today never comes back to it. If somebody's phone is giving trouble, sit with them while the rest look at the dashboard.

**Mention the idle warning** before it frightens somebody: if you sit idle too long the system warns you, then logs you out.

**Dashboard.** For each figure, say where it came from. Nobody types those numbers; they add up from what officers recorded.

**The menu.** Walk down it once: Members · Communications · Events & Minutes · Savings · Account Numbers · Savings Requests · Loans · Investments · Reports · Accounting · Dividends · Settings · Task Assignment · Data Migration · Security · Training · Help. Say that each officer sees only their own work, so their menus will differ — that is correct, not a fault.

Finish on **Help**: "before you call anybody, look here."

---

## Part 3 — Members (45 min)

**Show for 15 minutes, then let them work in pairs for 30.**

**Adding a member.** Go through the form, and stop on the three fields that decide what the member can have later:

- **Email** — without it there is no invitation, no login, and no personal account number.
- **Phone** — without it, no text message can reach them.
- **Bank, nominee, next of kin** — nobody thinks about these until a member dies or leaves the society. Then they are the only thing anybody cares about.

**Invitations.** One member from their page, or everybody who has not yet started, together. The member makes their own password. Say clearly: **we never know a member's password**, so never offer to log in for them.

**The readiness bar.** Call it "how much of the form is finished". Never let it sound like a mark against the member.

**Uploading many members.** Use our form. Say the warning twice: **do not move the columns around.** That single thing spoils most uploads.

**Savings-change requests.** A member asks from their phone to change their monthly savings. It arrives with a red number on the menu. Approving it changes what we expect from then on; it does not touch anything already recorded.

**Mistakes to name out loud**

- Adding the same person twice because the first record was hard to find. Search before you add.
- Putting the society's email on a member's record "so we can help them". It blocks their login and their statement.
- Quietly correcting a name months later. Statements already given out will no longer match, so say it in the meeting.

## Part 4 — Savings (60 min)

This is the most important hour of day one. Do not let it be cut short.

**A. One payment (10 min).** Record one payment from a member's page. Then say this and stop: *the member's savings went up, and in the same second the accounts showed the cash and what we now owe that member.* Do not open the accounts yet — day two does that.

**B. The monthly upload (15 min).** Show the form, the preview, and the name given to the upload. Say why we upload instead of typing: because it went in together, it can come out together.

**C. The bad upload (20 min).** This is the best twenty minutes of the day. Before the class, set the share percentage wrong.

1. Upload the list. Everything looks normal.
2. Open one member's statement. The savings and share split is wrong.
3. **Ask the room what they would do.** Somebody will say "delete it and do it again". Let that sit. Then say: if we could delete, a member's money could change and nobody's name would be on it.
4. Fix it in order: **correct the setting first** (if not, the next upload repeats the mistake) → open the upload → cancel it and type a real reason → upload the corrected list under a new name → open the same member's statement.
5. Say the two things that make officers comfortable doing this: cancelling writes the opposite of each line instead of rubbing anything out, and doing it twice by mistake does no harm.

**D. The check that matters (10 min).** Reports → Member Savings Control. Explain it in one question: *do all the members' savings added together equal the one line that says what we owe members?* If they ever differ, something is wrong, and month end will find it whether we looked or not.

**E. Members' own account numbers (5 min).** Only if it is switched on. Each member has a permanent account number in their own name, so we know who sent money without anybody sending a picture of a teller. Show the daily list: money we cannot yet match, money waiting to be used, and the tick that says both agree. The red number on the menu counts what needs a person; no number means nothing to do.

**Expect this question:** *what if a member sends more than they owe?* Whatever is left after the thing they named becomes their savings. They are saving the difference, not losing it.

## Part 5 — Loans (60 min)

**Run it as a role play, not a demonstration.** One loan, and each officer does their own step on their own laptop. If an office is not in the room, do that step yourself and say so.

**Put the line on the board first:**

> guarantors → Secretary → Treasurer → President → paid out

Say what it is: our own bye-laws, put inside the computer. Two rules to say plainly: **anybody can refuse, and must give a reason that is kept**, and **the Secretary cannot start until every guarantor has agreed.** Nobody can jump the line, and that is deliberate.

**Walk the steps**

1. **The request.** Member or officer chooses the type, the amount and how long. The system works out the repayments. The member accepts them and the terms. Show the alert the exco receives, because that is how officers learn there is work waiting.
2. **Guarantors.** Each guarantor answers for themselves, from their own phone or computer. Nobody agrees on a guarantor's behalf. Show the count against how many we require.
3. **Secretary — the checks.** Bank statement asked for and received; credit check done; repayment cover confirmed; salary confirmation for staff loans. Our rules may say a check is not needed for a certain loan type — that is recorded as a decision, not quietly skipped.
4. **Treasurer — the money side.** Can the society afford it, and can the member? Have the Treasurer open the member's savings and the loan report in another tab before acting. That habit is worth more than any screen.
5. **President — approval.** The last word. When it is approved, the money goes out and the record is written at once.
6. **A repayment.** Record one. Show how part of it reduces the loan and part is what we earned, and where the member sees it.
7. **The loan report.** Read one overdue line aloud and ask what they would actually do about that member on Monday morning.

**End on the loan's history:** every step, every name, the time, and what each person wrote. Tell them this is what they will be grateful for when somebody questions a decision a year from now.

**Mistakes to name**

- Approving just to see what happens. On the real system that pays out money.
- Refusing with an empty reason. The member and the next exco will both read that reason.
- Recording a repayment against the wrong member because the search was done by first name. Use the member number.

## Part 6 — Talking to members (20 min)

**Do this from a phone**, on the projector if you can, because that is where our members are.

**What the member sees:** savings, loans, repayment dates, statements, applying for a loan, guarantor requests, updating their details, and choosing what their transfers should pay for.

**How we reach them, cheapest first**

- **A message inside the system** — free, always.
- **A phone alert** — free, for members who installed our app.
- **A text message** — we pay for each one, so it goes **only** to members without the app. Every text is recorded with what happened to it. A member can switch texts off and still get everything else.
- **Email** — free, for members with an email address.

**One rule to leave them with:** always send to one test person before sending to the whole society. Members remember a bad group message for a long time.

## Part 7 — Big words, small meanings (20 min)

Day two starts here. Some officers are afraid of the word "accounting". Remove that fear in the first minute: nobody is being made into an accountant; we are only learning to read what the system already wrote.

Take the six words slowly, and use the room's own examples:

| Word | Say it like this |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | One record of one thing that happened to money |
| Debit and credit | The two sides of that record — what we got, and where it came from. They must be equal. |
| Ledger | All the entries about one thing, kept together |
| Balance | What is left after adding and subtracting |
| Trial balance | A quick test that both sides of everything still match |
| Reversal | Cancelling by writing the opposite. Nothing is rubbed out. |

Before moving on, ask one officer to explain "debit and credit" back to you in their own words. If they cannot, explain it again with money from somebody's pocket to somebody's hand.

---

## Part 8 — What the button wrote (40 min)

**Start on the board, not the screen.** Two sides, and they must be equal. Then the one idea that matters most in a cooperative:

> **Members' savings is money we are keeping for them. It is not our money.**

Money coming in does not make the society richer; it makes us owe more. An officer who understands only this asks better questions than most.

**Then the account lines** they will actually meet — go down the list once, in plain words. Do not read out the whole chart of accounts.

**Then three worked examples, with money.** Write each on the board, then find the same entry on the screen and match it line by line.

- **₦20,000 savings.** We hold ₦20,000 more; we owe the member ₦18,000 more, and ₦2,000 goes to share capital. Nothing was earned.
- **₦500,000 loan paid out.** The member owes the full ₦500,000, but only ₦490,000 leaves the bank: ₦5,000 form fee (ours) and ₦5,000 insurance (not ours — we hold it for the insurance company).
- **₦50,000 repayment.** The loan comes down ₦40,000 and we earned ₦10,000. The rest is our own money coming home.

**Ask before you answer.** On the loan example, ask "how much does this member owe?" and wait for the wrong answer. It teaches better than the right one.

**Two mistakes to dwell on**, because they make us look richer than we are: calling insurance our income, and calling a whole repayment profit. Both end the same way — we promise a dividend the society cannot pay. Ask whether it has ever happened here. The room usually has a story.

---

## Part 9 — Correcting mistakes (35 min)

**Lead with the rule:** we never delete, we cancel.

**Cancel one entry in front of them** and make three things visible:

1. It asks for a reason, and the reason is kept with your name.
2. Both records stay — the first one and the cancellation. We add to history; we never rewrite it.
3. The member's record and the accounts are corrected together, so they can never disagree.

Then let everybody cancel one themselves. An officer who has done it once calmly will do it correctly when it matters.

**If the system refuses to cancel something**, it has no safe way to undo it. Say plainly: a refusal is the system protecting us, not a fault to work around.

**Money that arrived but is not yet used.** Teach the idea even if the account-number feature is off, because it is how we should think about all unexplained money:

- It is recorded the moment it lands. We owe the member from that moment.
- **Using** it — for savings, a loan, or Target Advance — is a separate decision, and that decision can be changed later.
- Two refusals: the system never guesses which member sent money, and it never pretends the money did not arrive. You also cannot use more than arrived.

**Expect this question:** *what if we cancel something the member has already seen?* Cancel it anyway, then tell the member what changed and why. A record that shows both the mistake and the correction is our protection, not our shame.

## Part 10 — End of month (50 min)

Everybody works along on the practice copy. Read a step, let them do it, compare answers, then move on.

**The six checks, in this order — the order matters:**

1. **What is in each bank account**, and how it moved.
2. **Our cash record against the bank statement.** A difference is either timing or a mistake. Find out which.
3. **Members' savings against what we say we owe members.** These must be the same.
4. **The loan list** — who is behind, and who must be chased.
5. **The trial balance, then the statements.** Both sides must match before anybody reads anything.
6. **Close the month.**

**What closing does:** nobody can add anything into a closed month afterwards. That is what makes a report we handed the committee in March still say the same thing in September. If something truly must be corrected, we correct it in the open month so everybody can see what was done and when.

**Get each table to report one thing that does not agree**, and work one of them out together in front of everybody. A room that has found and explained a difference on the practice copy will not panic when it meets a real one.

**Tell the President plainly:** checks three and six are where their attention belongs. Those are the two the society gets questioned about.

## Part 11 — Sharing the profit (30 min)

**The steps** (Accounting → Dividends): choose the period → enter the splits for dividend, reserve, honorarium and anything else → set the borrowing-based share if we use one → Compute → read the member list → the President declares → save or print the paper for the committee file.

**Explain the borrowing-based share in one sentence:** members who borrowed more brought us more interest, so part of the profit can follow that instead of savings alone. Whether we do it, and by how much, is our decision — not something to guess at in the class.

**The three rules, said as rules**

- **Check first.** Do the month-end checks before computing anything. Profit that has not been checked is a promise we may not be able to keep.
- **The meeting decides.** The committee or AGM approves it outside the system. The computer records the decision; it does not make it.
- **Correct, do not edit.** A wrong declaration is fixed with a fresh entry. Nobody goes back into what was already declared.

**Have the President compute one and explain each split to the room before declaring.** If a split cannot be explained here, it is far better to find that out now than at the AGM.

---

## Part 12 — Staying out of trouble (25 min)

**Ask it as a challenge: when the auditor asks, can you show it in one minute?** Then show each one live:

| They ask | You open |
| --- | --- |
| "Show me everything for this period" | Reports → General Ledger Register, then export |
| "Explain this figure" | Accounting → the account → click into it |
| "Who approved this loan?" | The loan's history — step, name, time, comment |
| "Why was this upload changed?" | The cancelled upload and the reason typed on it |
| "Did the member get informed?" | The message record and the text-message record |

**Sharing out duties.** Go back to Task Assignment, now that they understand day two. The person who records money and the person who approves it should not be the same person. Look at our own present arrangement and ask whether it still makes sense.

**Five rules that protect officers**

- Everybody uses the six-digit phone code. No exception for senior officers.
- One person, one login. Never shared — your name is on everything you do.
- When an officer leaves office, close the account that same day.
- Never test anything on real member records.
- The President can never be locked out, so access can always be restored.

---

## Part 13 — Everyone tries alone (15 min)

Use the sign-off sheet in §7 of the lesson plan. Watch each officer do their three tasks without help. Do not coach while they are working; note where they hesitate and cover it in the closing.

**Close with the three sentences you opened with:** one place for everything, nothing rubbed out, we decide who does what. Then the last instruction: this week, open the system and do one real piece of your own work in it. If they wait a month, they will have forgotten.

---

## Optional part — Target Advance (45 min)

Only if our society runs the scheme. It stays hidden in the menu otherwise.

**Explain the scheme before you show any screen.** Everybody contributes the same amount each period, and a ballot decides who collects and in which period. After you collect, you keep contributing until the round ends. Part of your payout is your own money; the society adds the rest and takes it back from what you still have to pay.

**Running one round:** agree the plan (amount × number of periods = the target) → create the round with real dates → record the money set aside and what the society is willing to add → open joining → each application passes three separate checks → close joining and run the ballot → each period, take out the list of what is due, collect it, and bring the confirmed amounts back in → pay each member on their balloted turn.

**What officers get wrong**

- A member cannot join until they accept the rules. An officer may record a paper acceptance, and that recording is kept.
- The three checks are meant to be done by **different people**. Splitting them is the whole point.
- If there will not be enough money, the ballot is blocked until it is settled or an officer accepts the gap. That block is protecting our cash.
- The scheme's money is recorded like all our other money, and checked the same way at month end.

## Questions you will be asked

**"Can I delete it?"** No, and that is on purpose. Cancel it, and say why.

**"Can I record something for last month?"** Until the month is closed, yes. After it is closed, no — do it in the open month.

**"Why can't I see that menu?"** Because it is not part of your duties. If it should be, that is a Task Assignment change, not a fault.

**"The member says they paid but it is not showing."** Check the account-number list for money we could not match, then the savings record. Never record a payment on the strength of a picture of a teller alone.

**"Do members get told automatically?"** Inside the system and on their phone, yes. Text messages only for members without the app, and only if we have credit. Email, if it is set up.

**"What if two officers act on the same loan at once?"** Only the officer whose turn it is can act, and the history shows who did what. The line cannot be jumped.

**"Can we change who approves what?"** Yes — Settings → Task Assignment. It is our decision, and it takes effect immediately.
