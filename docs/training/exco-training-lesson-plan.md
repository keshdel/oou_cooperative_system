# Exco Training — Lesson Plan

**Course:** Running our society on the computer
**Who it is for:** All exco members — President, Secretary, Treasurer, and every other exco officer
**How long:** Two half-days, about 3½ hours each, one week apart
**How it is taught:** A facilitator in front, a projector, and everybody logged in on their own laptop
**Other materials:** the teaching slides, the [facilitator notes](exco-training-facilitator-notes.md), the [user manual](../user-manual.md), and Help in the side menu

---

## 1. Why we are doing this

Before the system, our records lived in three places — a savings book, a loan file, and somebody's notebook. They only agreed after somebody sat down for a whole evening to make them agree.

Now there is one place. Every officer works in the same system, and the accounts are written at the same moment the officer records something. Nothing is ever rubbed out.

For that to work, every officer needs to know two things: **which screen is mine**, and **what happened to the money when I pressed that button**. Day one teaches the first. Day two teaches the second.

**A note on language.** Officers come from all walks of life. Nobody here needs accounting training or big grammar. Every hard word is explained in ordinary language the first time it appears, and the facilitator should stop and explain again the moment a face says otherwise.

## 2. What officers can do afterwards

**Everybody, by the end of day one**

- Log in safely, set up the six-digit phone code, and find any page
- Say what their own office is allowed to do, and where that is decided
- Look up any member, read a savings statement, and open the loan list
- Follow a loan from request to payment, and say who is delaying it
- Know which of their actions reaches the member, and what it costs us

**Everybody, by the end of day two**

- Explain what the system wrote when savings, a loan, or a repayment was recorded
- Correct a mistake the right way — cancel it with a reason, never delete
- Work through the six end-of-month checks
- Explain what closing a month means, and why it matters
- Explain how profit is shared, and what must be agreed before anybody presses Declare

**Each office, on its own**

| Office | Must be able to do without help |
| --- | --- |
| President | Approve a loan at the last step; declare a dividend; close a month; share out duties in Task Assignment; open and close staff accounts |
| Secretary | Add and edit members; send invitations; do the loan checks; record meetings; send messages to members |
| Treasurer | Take savings and repayments; upload the month; check the loan side of a loan; correct entries; do the end-of-month checks |
| Exco member | Read the member list, savings, loans and reports; sit in an approval meeting with the real figures in front of them |

## 3. Before the class

**The facilitator prepares**

1. A **practice copy** of the system with pretend members — never teach on real records. Make sure no real member can receive a message from it.
2. A **login for each officer**, in their real office. Then what they can and cannot open in class is exactly what they will meet at their desk.
3. One **member login**, to show the member's own side on a phone.
4. Printed copies of the practice sheet in §6 and the sign-off sheet in §7.

**Officers bring**

- A laptop, and the phone that will hold their six-digit code
- Their own real questions. The problems in their own record-keeping are the best teaching material in the room.

**Check before day two:** everybody did the homework in §6. Day two is built on it.

## 4. Day one — the work you do every week

*The screens you touch, and who is allowed to touch them.*

| # | Time | Part | What they should get | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0:00–0:30 | **Why we moved** | What each office may do, and where that is decided | Discussion, then open Settings → Task Assignment |
| 2 | 0:30–0:50 | **Logging in and looking around** | Log in, six-digit code, the dashboard, the menu | Everybody on their own laptop |
| 3 | 0:50–1:35 | **Members** | Add a member, send the invitation, read the readiness bar | Show once, then work in pairs |
| 4 | 1:35–2:35 | **Savings** | Take a payment, upload the month, fix a bad upload | Show a mistake on purpose, then everybody practises |
| — | 2:35–2:50 | *Break* | | |
| 5 | 2:50–3:50 | **Loans** | Take one loan from guarantors to payment | Role play — each officer does their own step |
| 6 | 3:50–4:10 | **Talking to members** | The four ways to reach a member, and what each costs | Show it from a phone |
| 7 | 4:10–4:20 | **Close** | Homework and questions | Questions |

### What is inside each part

**1. Why we moved (30 min).** The problem we had. The three things that are true now: one place for everything, nothing is rubbed out, and we decide who does what. Then open Task Assignment so officers see their own duties on the screen instead of only hearing about them.

**2. Logging in and looking around (20 min).** Log in, change the password, set the six-digit phone code, and meet the idle warning before it surprises somebody. Then the dashboard — for every figure, say where it came from. Then the menu as a map of our work.

**3. Members (45 min).** Adding a member, and the three fields that decide what the member can have later: email, phone, and the bank/nominee/next-of-kin details. Invitations, one at a time and many at once. The readiness bar. Uploading many members with our form — and why moving the columns spoils it. Savings-change requests: a member asks from their phone, an officer approves, and it only changes what is expected from then on.

**4. Savings (60 min).** Taking one payment, and the sentence to remember: *the member's savings goes up, and at the same second the accounts show the cash and what we now owe that member.* Then the monthly upload, and why a whole upload can be cancelled together.
**The set piece:** run an upload with the share percentage set wrong, then fix it properly — correct the setting first, cancel the upload with a reason, upload again under a new name, then open one member's statement to see it with your own eyes.
Finish on the report that compares members' savings with what the accounts say we owe.
If members have their own account numbers, show the daily list: money we cannot yet match, money waiting to be used, and the tick that says the two agree.

**5. Loans (60 min).** The line our bye-laws lay down: **guarantors → Secretary → Treasurer → President → paid out.** Anybody can refuse, and must give a reason that is kept. The Secretary cannot start until every guarantor has agreed.
Each officer in the room does their own step on one real-looking application. Then the Secretary's checks (bank statement, credit check, repayment cover, salary confirmation), the payment, recording a repayment, and the loan report showing who is behind. End by opening the loan's history, which shows every step and every name.

**6. Talking to members (20 min).** What the member sees on their phone. Then how we reach them, cheapest first: a message inside the system and a phone alert are free; a text message costs us and goes only to members without the app; email is free. Every text is recorded, and a member can switch texts off and still get everything else.

## 5. Day two — what happens to the money

*Every button writes something. Here is what it writes, and how you check it.*

| # | Time | Part | What they should get | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | 0:00–0:20 | **Big words, small meanings** | Six words explained in ordinary language | Board, with the room's own examples |
| 8 | 0:20–1:00 | **What the button wrote** | Three everyday entries, with real amounts | Board first, then the same entry on screen |
| 9 | 1:00–1:35 | **Correcting mistakes** | Cancel an entry properly | Show once, then everybody does one |
| — | 1:35–1:50 | *Break* | | |
| 10 | 1:50–2:40 | **End of month** | The six checks, in order | Everybody works along on the practice copy |
| 11 | 2:40–3:10 | **Sharing the profit** | Compute a dividend and explain every split | Show, then discuss our own policy |
| 12 | 3:10–3:35 | **Staying out of trouble** | Show an auditor anything in a minute | Show live, then the safety rules |
| 13 | 3:35–3:50 | **Everyone tries alone** | Three tasks each | Sign-off sheet, §7 |

### What is inside each part

**7. Big words, small meanings (20 min).** Say at the start that nobody is being turned into an accountant. Then six words, each in one line: entry, debit and credit, ledger, balance, trial balance, reversal. Ask somebody to say "debit and credit" back in their own words before moving on. Everything else that day is these six words in different clothes.

**8. What the button wrote (40 min).** The one big idea first: **members' savings is money we are keeping for them; it is not our money.** When savings come in, the society is not richer — it owes more.
Then the few account lines officers actually meet:

| Number | Name on the screen | In plain words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1000 | Cash and Bank | Money we are holding |
| 1100 | Loans Receivable | Money members are owing us |
| 2000 | Member Deposits | Money we are owing members |
| 2010 | Unallocated Receipts | Money that came, not yet used |
| 2110 | Insurance Payable | Insurance money we hold for the insurance company |
| 3200 | Share Capital | The share part of each payment |
| 4000 | Loan Interest Income | What we earned from lending |
| 4100 | Fee Income | Form fees and late fees |
| 5000 | Operating Expenses | What we spent to run the society |

Then three worked examples with real amounts:

- **A member pays ₦20,000 savings.** We now hold ₦20,000 more cash; we owe that member ₦18,000 more savings and ₦2,000 goes to share capital. Nothing was earned.
- **A ₦500,000 loan is paid out.** The member owes the full ₦500,000, but only ₦490,000 leaves the bank: ₦5,000 is our form fee, and ₦5,000 is insurance we are holding for the insurance company. The fee is ours. The insurance is not.
- **The member repays ₦50,000.** We received ₦50,000, the loan comes down by ₦40,000, and only ₦10,000 is what we earned. The rest is our own money coming home.

Finish by opening one of those entries on screen and matching it line by line.

**9. Correcting mistakes (35 min).** The rule: **we never delete, we cancel.** Cancelling asks for a reason, keeps both records, and corrects the member's record and the accounts at the same time. If the system refuses to cancel something, it has no safe way to undo it — the refusal is protecting us.
Then money that arrived but has not been used: it is recorded the moment it lands because we owe the member from that moment, and deciding what it pays for is a separate step that can be changed later. Two refusals worth remembering: the system never guesses which member sent money, and it never pretends the money did not arrive.

**10. End of month (50 min).** Work the six checks together, in order: bank accounts → our cash record against the bank statement → members' savings against what we say we owe → the loan list → the trial balance and statements → close the month. Explain what closing does, and that a correction to a closed month is made in the open month so everybody can see it.

**11. Sharing the profit (30 min).** Choose the period, enter the splits (dividend, reserve, honorarium, anything else), set the borrowing-based share if we use one, compute, read the member list, and the President declares. Save the paper for the committee file.
The three rules: check before computing; the committee or AGM decides outside the system; and correct a mistake with a fresh entry, never by editing what was declared.

**12. Staying out of trouble (25 min).** What an auditor asks for and where each answer lives. Then the five safety rules: separate recording from approving, everybody uses the phone code, one person one login, close a leaver's account the same day, and never test on real records.

**Optional part — Target Advance (45 min).** Only if our society runs the scheme. Everybody contributes the same amount each period and a ballot decides who collects when. Cover joining and the three separate checks, the ballot and what blocks it, collecting and paying, and how the money is recorded. Run it after part 11 or as a separate small class.

## 6. Practice

All on the practice copy, at the officer's own laptop.

- **Practice 1 — Add and invite.** Add a member with full details, send the invitation, and get the readiness bar to full. *(Secretary leads; everybody does it.)*
- **Practice 2 — The bad upload.** Upload the prepared list, see that the split is wrong, and put it right in the correct order. Then say what you would tell the member who already saw the wrong statement. *(Treasurer leads.)*
- **Practice 3 — One loan, four officers.** Take one loan from guarantors to payment, each officer doing their own step, then record the first repayment. *(Everybody.)*
- **Practice 4 — Find the entry.** Open the entry behind that repayment and read both sides aloud. *(Everybody.)*
- **Practice 5 — Cancel it.** Cancel that repayment with a reason, then show where the reason appears afterwards. *(Treasurer and President.)*
- **Practice 6 — End of month.** Do the six checks and report one thing that does not agree. *(Treasurer leads; President watches what is being certified.)*
- **Practice 7 — Declare.** Compute a dividend on the practice figures and explain each split to the room before declaring. *(President.)*
- **Homework between the two days.** On the real system: find your own member record, read your own savings statement, and open the loan list. Bring one question about anything you could not explain.

## 7. Sign-off

An officer is signed off when they finish their own row without help, with the facilitator watching. Keep the sheet with the training record.

| Officer | Task 1 | Task 2 | Task 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| President | Approve a loan at the last step | Compute and declare a dividend | Close a month and explain why |
| Secretary | Add a member and send the invitation | Do the loan checks and pass it on | Record a meeting |
| Treasurer | Upload savings, then cancel it with a reason | Record a repayment and find its entry | Check members' savings against the accounts |
| Exco member | Read a member's savings statement | Read the loan report | Say who is delaying a pending loan |

Nobody fails. If somebody cannot finish a task, sit with them for thirty minutes on that one task within two weeks.

## 8. After the class

- **Help** in the side menu answers most day-to-day questions. Look there before calling anybody.
- **Training** in the side menu keeps these slides and this plan on hand for anyone who wants to go over them again.
- Leave the practice copy open for a month, so officers can rehearse anything that frightens them before doing it for real.
- At the next exco meeting, ask what has gone wrong since, and who needs a small refresher.
- Run this course again for every new exco after an election. The system is only as good as the officers' confidence in it.
