Day 104

Part 1 · Why we moved

A member says their savings is wrong. How do we know who is right?

Before, the answer was in three places — the savings book, the loan file, and somebody's notebook. They only agreed after somebody sat down for hours.

Ask the question and keep quiet. Let two or three officers answer. Their own answers describe the problem better than any slide.

Day 105

Part 1

Three things that are true now

01

One place for everything

We all work in the same system. When you record something, the accounts are updated the same second. Nobody has to match books at month end.

02

Nothing is rubbed out

You cannot delete. To correct a mistake, you cancel it — and the system asks you why. That protects you as much as it protects the member.

03

We decide who does what

What the Treasurer can open, and what the Secretary can open, is our decision. We can change it any day.

These three sentences hold the whole course together. Point back to them later by number: the bad upload is number two, Task Assignment is number three.

Day 107

Part 2 · Logging in

Your password is the key to the society's money

  1. Log in, and change the password you were given.
  2. Click your name → Security & 2FA → point your phone at the picture on the screen → type the six numbers it shows.
  3. If you sit idle too long, the system warns you and logs you out. That is on purpose.

Now nobody can enter with your password alone. They would need your phone too.

Never share your login. Every action carries your name, so if two people use one login, nobody can be held responsible.

Everybody does this now, on their own laptop, with you walking round. Help anyone whose phone gives trouble — the officer who skips it today will never come back to it.

Day 109

Part 3 · Members

Three things to get right when you add a member

Email

Without it, the member cannot get their invitation, cannot log in, and cannot be given their own account number.

Phone number

Without it, no text message can reach them.

Bank, next of kin, nominee

Nobody thinks about these until a member dies or leaves. Then they are the only thing that matters.

The member makes their own password from the invitation. We never know a member's password — so never offer to log in for them.

Show adding a member, then the invitation (one member, and many at once), then the readiness bar. Explain readiness as "how much of the form is complete", not as a score on the member.

Day 110

Part 3 · Common mistakes

Three habits to drop today

  • Search first  Adding the same person twice because you could not find them the first time.
  • Their own email  Putting the society's email on a member's record "so we can help them". It blocks their login and their statement.
  • Say it out  Quietly correcting a name months later. Statements already given out will no longer match.

Uploading many members at once? Use the form we provide and do not move the columns around. That is what spoils most uploads.

Cover savings-change requests here too: a member asks from their phone to change their monthly savings, it arrives with a red count on the menu, and approving it only changes what is expected going forward.

Day 111

Part 4 · Savings

One payment, two records, at the same time

The member's savings goes up — and in the same second the society's records show cash received and the money we now owe that member.

You will never again have to make two books agree by hand. That is the whole reason we moved.

Record one payment from a member's page, then say the sentence above and stop. Do not open the accounts yet — day two does that. You are planting the idea.

Day 112

Part 4 · The monthly upload

The whole month goes in together

Instead of typing each member, we upload the month's list in one go. The upload gets one name, like JULY-SALARY.

Because it went in together, it can come out together. That is why uploading beats typing.

Show the form, the preview, and the name given to the batch. Keep it short — the next slide is where the real learning happens.

Day 113

Part 4 · When it goes wrong

The upload was wrong. What now?

StepDo thisWhy this order
1Fix the setting that caused itIf not, the next upload repeats the same mistake
2Open the upload → cancel it, and say whyYour reason is kept, so nobody can wonder later
3Upload the corrected list, with a new nameThe two uploads never get confused
4Open one member's statementSee it with your own eyes before telling anybody

Cancelling does not rub anything out. It writes the opposite of each line, so balances go back to what they were, and doing it twice by mistake does no harm.

Set the share-capital percentage wrong before the class. Run the upload, open a statement, then ask the room what they would do. Somebody will say "delete it" — let that sit, then explain: if we could delete, a member's money could change with nobody's name on it.

Day 114

The bad upload

Upload the list we prepared. You will see the savings and share money split wrongly. Now put it right, in the correct order.

Then say out loud: what would you tell the member who already saw the wrong statement?

Day 115

Part 4 · The check that matters

Do the members' savings add up to what we say we owe?

Add every member's savings together. Then look at the one line in our accounts that says how much we owe members. The two must be the same number.

The report that shows this is Reports → Member Savings Control. If the two ever differ, something is wrong — and month end will find it whether we looked or not.

This is the most useful report an exco can learn to read. It comes back on day two as step three of the month-end list.

Day 116

Part 4 · Members' own account numbers

Money can arrive before anyone tells us

STEP 1 · IT LANDS

We owe the member from that moment

The system records the money at once and holds it for that member — even before anybody decides what it is for.

STEP 2 · WE USE IT

What it pays for is a separate decision

Savings, loan, or Target Advance. And it can be changed later, because deciding is not the same as receiving.

Each member has a permanent account number in their own name. So we know who sent money without anybody sending a picture of a teller.

Only if member account numbers are switched on. Show the daily list: money we cannot yet match to a member, money waiting to be used, and the tick that says both lists agree.

Day 117

Part 4 · Two useful refusals

Two things the system will not do

It will not guess a member

If it cannot tell who sent money, it holds it aside and waits for a person to say. Guessing would put one member's money in another member's account.

It will not pretend money never came

The money really entered our bank. Only our decision about it can be changed — and then it goes back to waiting.

You also cannot use more than what arrived, so the holding pot can never go below zero.

Expect: "what if a member sends more than they owe?" Whatever is left after the thing they named simply becomes their savings. They are saving the difference, not losing it.

Day 224

Part 7 · Big words, small meanings

Six words people use to confuse you

WordWhat it really means
EntryOne record of one thing that happened to money
Debit & creditThe two sides of that record. One side says what we got; the other says where it came from. The two sides must be equal.
LedgerAll the entries for one thing, kept together — like one page per matter
BalanceWhat is left after adding and subtracting everything
Trial balanceA quick test: add all the left sides and all the right sides. They must match.
ReversalCancelling an entry by writing its opposite. Nothing is rubbed out.

That is the whole language. Everything else today is these six words in different clothes.

Go slowly here and use the room's own examples. Ask somebody to explain "debit and credit" back to you in their own words before you move on.

Day 225

Part 7 · The one big idea

Members' savings is money we are keeping for them. It is not our money.

When savings come in, the society does not become richer. It simply owes more. An officer who understands only this already asks better questions than most.

Board, not screen. Do not move on until at least one officer has said this back to you in their own words.

Day 229

Part 8 · A member repays

₦50,000 repayment — but we only earned ₦10,000

Money received

Cash and Bank  +₦50,000

What it means

Loan owed comes down  ₦40,000
We earned (interest)  ₦10,000

Only the ₦10,000 interest is income. The ₦40,000 is just our own money coming back home.

This is the line most often confused in exco meetings, and it decides how much can honestly be shared at the end of the year. Practice 4 fits here: everybody opens the entry behind their own repayment from yesterday and reads both sides aloud.

Day 230

Part 8 · Two costly mistakes

Two errors that make us look richer than we are

Calling insurance our money

It belongs to the insurance company. If we count it as profit, we share money that is not ours — and we still have to pay it.

Calling the whole repayment profit

Most of a repayment is our own money returning. Only the interest is profit.

Both mistakes end the same way: we promise members a dividend the society cannot pay.

Ask whether this has ever happened here before. The room usually has a story, and the story teaches better than the slide.

Day 231

Part 9 · Correcting mistakes

What happens when you cancel an entry

01

It asks you why

You must type a reason, and it is kept with your name on it.

02

Both stay

The first entry and the cancellation both remain. We add to history; we never rewrite it.

03

Everything moves together

The member's record and the society's accounts are corrected at the same time, so they can never disagree.

Sometimes the system refuses to cancel. That means it has no safe way to undo that thing — the refusal is protecting us.

Cancel one entry in front of them, then let everybody do practice 5 themselves. An officer who has done it once calmly will do it correctly under pressure.

Day 232

Part 10 · End of month

Six checks, in this order

  1. What is in each bank account, and how it moved.
  2. Our cash record against the bank statement. Any difference is either timing or an error — find out which.
  3. Members' savings against what we say we owe members. These must be the same.
  4. The loan list — who is behind, and who must be chased.
  5. The trial balance, then the statements. Both sides must match before we read anything.
  6. Close the month.

Do them in this order. Each one prepares the next.

Read a step, everybody does it, compare answers, then move on. In practice 6 each table reports one thing that does not agree, and you work one of them out together.

Day 233

Part 10 · Closing the month

A report printed in March must still say the same thing in September

When we close a month, nobody can add anything back into it. That is what makes a report we gave the committee trustworthy months later.

If something really must be corrected in a closed month, we correct it in the open month, so everybody can see what was done and when.

Tell the President plainly: checks three and six are where their attention belongs, because those are the two the society will be questioned about.

Day 234

Part 11 · Sharing the profit

From profit to money in members' accounts

StepWhat you do
1Choose the period — from which date to which date
2Say how the profit is split: dividend, reserve, honorarium, anything else
3If part is to be shared by how much each member borrowed, put that percentage
4Press Compute, then read the list member by member
5The President declares it — every member's account is credited

Step 3 in plain words: members who borrowed more brought us more interest, so part of the profit can follow that, not savings alone. Whether we do it, and by how much, is our decision, not the computer's.

Practice 7 fits here: the President computes a declaration and explains each split to the room before declaring. If a split cannot be explained here, better to find out now than at the AGM.

Day 235

Part 11 · Before anybody presses Declare

Three rules

Check first

Do the month-end checks before computing anything. Profit that has not been checked is a promise we may not be able to keep.

The meeting decides

The committee or the AGM approves it outside the system. The computer only records the decision; it does not make it.

Fix by correction, not by editing

If a declaration is wrong, we correct it with a fresh entry. Nobody goes back to change what was already declared.

If the society's own policy on the splits is unsettled, write it on the board for the exco meeting and move on. Do not let the class turn into a bye-laws argument.

Day 237

Part 12 · Keeping everybody safe

Five simple rules

  • Separate  The person who records money and the person who approves it should not be the same person.
  • Phone code  Every officer uses the six-digit phone code. No exception for big offices.
  • One person, one login  Never share. Your name is on everything you do.
  • Leavers  When an officer leaves office, close their account that same day.
  • Practice copy  Never test anything on real member records.

Open the society's own Task Assignment and ask whether the present sharing of duties still makes sense. Remind them the President can never be locked out, so access can always be restored.

Optional39

Only if our society runs it

Target Advance — the ajo, on the system

Everybody pays the same amount each period. A ballot decides who collects, and in which period. After you collect, you keep paying till the round ends. Part of your payout is your own money; the society adds the rest and takes it back from what you still have to pay.

  • A member cannot join until they accept the rules. An officer can record a paper acceptance, and that is kept.
  • Three different people check a member before joining. Splitting it that way is the whole point.
  • If there will not be enough money, the ballot is blocked until it is settled. That block is protecting us.
  • The money in the scheme is recorded like every other money we hold, and checked the same way.

Skip this if the module is off — it does not even show in the menu. Run it after part 11, or as a separate small class for the officers who will operate it.
