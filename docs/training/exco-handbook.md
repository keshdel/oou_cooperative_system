# The Exco Handbook

**Running our society on the computer — the book you keep**

**Who it is for:** every exco officer — President, Secretary, Treasurer, and every other officer
**How to use it:** you do not read it end to end. Find the job you are doing, and follow the steps.
**Other material:** the [lesson plan](exco-training-lesson-plan.md) and [facilitator notes](exco-training-facilitator-notes.md) are for whoever teaches the class. The [user manual](../user-manual.md) is the short reference. This book is yours.

**About the words in this book.** Officers come from all walks of life. Nobody needs accounting training to use this system. Every hard word is explained in ordinary language the first time it appears. If a word is not explained, that is our fault, not yours — tell us and we will fix the book.

**About the amounts.** Every example uses real-looking money, because "debit the receivable" teaches nobody anything and "the member owes us ₦50,000 more than before" teaches everybody.

# Part 1 — Before you start

## 1. What this system is

Before, our records lived in three places: a savings book, a loan file, and somebody's notebook. They agreed only after somebody sat down for a whole evening to make them agree.

Now there is one place. Every officer works in the same system. The accounts are written at the very moment you record something, by the system, not by you. Nothing is ever rubbed out.

Two things follow from that, and they matter more than any button in this book:

- **You do not have to be an accountant.** When you record a savings payment, the system writes the accounting for you. Part 6 teaches you to *read* what it wrote, so you can check it. That is all.
- **Nothing is deleted, ever.** A mistake is corrected by writing an opposite entry with a reason. The mistake stays visible, and so does the correction. That is what makes the records trustworthy — and what protects you personally, because anyone can see what you did and why.

## 2. Getting in

**Your first time**

1. You will get an email inviting you in. Open it and click the link.
2. Choose a password. The society sets how long and how strong it must be — the screen tells you.
3. You are asked to set up a **six-digit code**. Do it. It takes two minutes and it is the single thing that stops somebody else using your name.

**The six-digit code (two-factor)**

This is a number that changes every thirty seconds on your own phone. Even somebody who steals your password cannot get in without your phone.

1. Install an authenticator app on your phone — Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator, both free.
2. In CoopMS, open your account menu and choose **Security**.
3. Point your phone's app at the square barcode on the screen.
4. Type the six digits it shows, to prove it worked.
5. **Write down the backup codes it gives you and keep them at home.** They are how you get in if your phone is lost or broken.

**Every time after that:** your email, your password, then the six digits from your phone.

**If you are locked out**

- *Forgot your password* — click "Forgot password" on the login screen.
- *Lost your phone* — use one of the backup codes you wrote down.
- *Lost both* — ask the President. Only the President can reset another officer's six-digit code, and they should be looking at your face when they do it.

## 3. Finding your way around

The menu runs down the left side. What you see depends on your office (see §4).

- **Dashboard** — the front page. Totals for members, savings, loans, and what needs attention today.
- **Members** — everybody in the society.
- **Savings** — money members have put in.
- **Loans** — requests, approvals, and money owed.
- **Accounting** — the books. Where every entry lives.
- **Reports** — the statements and lists you print for a meeting.
- **Communications** — messages to members.
- **Governance** — meetings, minutes, attendance.
- **Settings** — how the society is set up. Mostly the President.
- **Training** — the course, and this book.

**Two things worth knowing early**

- Wherever you see an amount, you can usually click it to see what it is made of.
- Almost every list has an **Export** button, which gives you the same list as a spreadsheet.

## 4. What your office is allowed to do

The system does not guess. Each office is given a list of duties, and each duty opens certain screens.

Out of the box:

| Office | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| **President** | Everything. Approves the last step of a loan, declares a dividend, closes a month, shares out duties, opens and closes officer logins. |
| **Secretary** | Adds and edits members, sends invitations, does the loan checks, records meetings, sends messages. |
| **Treasurer** | Takes savings and repayments, uploads the month, checks the loan figures, corrects entries, does the end-of-month checks. |
| **Exco member** | Reads members, savings, loans and reports. Sits in a meeting with the real figures in front of them. |

This is a starting point, not a rule. The President can change it — see §9.

**The President always has everything, and cannot be locked out.** Somebody must always be able to put access back after a mistake.

# Part 2 — Setting up the society

*Do this once, at the beginning. Mostly the President, with the Treasurer for the money rules.*

## 5. Our name, our details

**Settings → General**

Fill in the society's registered name, registration number, address, telephone and email. Upload the logo.

This is not decoration. This name and logo appear on every statement, every receipt, every loan letter and every email a member receives. Get it right once and everything the society sends out looks like the society.

Also set here:

- **Financial year start month** — the month our year begins. Reports use it.
- **Currency** and **date format**.
- **Session timeout** — how many idle minutes before the system logs you out. Shorter is safer.

## 6. Our rules for savings

**Settings → Savings**

| Setting | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum monthly savings | The least a member may save each month. |
| Savings due day | The day of the month savings are expected. |
| Late fee percentage | Charged when a payment is recorded after the due day. This is society income, and it does **not** increase the member's savings. |
| Share capital percent | How much of every contribution becomes the member's share of the society rather than their savings. |

**Read the share capital line twice.** If you set it to 5, then a member paying ₦10,000 has ₦9,500 added to savings and ₦500 recorded as their share of the society. Both belong to the member. But their savings balance will read ₦9,500, not ₦10,000, and if nobody told them they will think money went missing. **Decide this before you record a single payment, and tell the members what you decided.** If your society does not use share capital, set it to 0 and the whole contribution stays as savings.

## 7. Our rules for loans

**Settings → Loans**

| Setting | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Loan multiplier | How many times their savings a member may borrow. Set 2, and a member with ₦500,000 saved may borrow ₦1,000,000. |
| Minimum savings for a loan | Below this, no loan. |
| Minimum membership months | How long they must have been a member. |
| Maximum tenure | The longest repayment period, in months. |
| Interest rate, per loan type | What we charge. |
| Insurance rate | Withheld when the loan is paid out, and held for the insurer. It is **not** our income. |
| Application fee | Charged when the loan is paid out. This one **is** our income. |
| Guarantors required | How many members must stand for the borrower. |

A borrower of ₦500,000 with 1% insurance and 1% fee receives ₦490,000. The society holds ₦5,000 for the insurer and earns ₦5,000. Say this out loud to members before the first loan, or the first borrower will believe they were short-changed.

## 8. Officers and their logins

**Settings → System Users**

1. **Add User**.
2. Name, email, and their office.
3. Save. They get an invitation email.

Rules worth keeping:

- **One login per person. Never share.** Every action is recorded against a name. A shared login means nobody can be held responsible and nobody can be cleared.
- **When an officer leaves office, switch their login off the same day.** Switch off, do not delete — their past actions must remain readable.
- Set the **password policy** under Settings → Password Policy, and require the six-digit code for staff.

## 9. Sharing out the duties

**Settings → Task Assignment**

This is where the President decides what each office can open. Duties are grouped: Members, Savings, Loans, Accounting, Reports, Governance and so on.

Two levels:

1. **By office** — every Treasurer can do this.
2. **By person** — this one named officer can do this, whatever their office says. Use it sparingly, and write down why in the minutes.

If an officer says "it says I am not allowed", this is the screen that decides it.

## 10. Email and text messages

**Settings → Mail** and **Settings → Text messages**

Emails carry statements, invitations and loan letters. Text messages reach members who do not use the app.

- Put in the sending details your provider gave you.
- Press **Send test** before you trust it. A wrong setting here is silent — the system thinks it sent, and the member never hears.
- Text messages cost money per message. Members using the phone app get alerts free.

## 11. Our list of accounts

**Accounting → Chart of Accounts**

This is the list of pots the money sits in. The society starts with a standard list, and you add to it.

The codes are grouped:

| Code | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| 1000s | Things we own — bank accounts, cash, money members owe us, investments |
| 2000s | Things we owe — members' savings above all |
| 3000s | The society's own worth — accumulated surplus, share capital |
| 4000s | Money we earn — loan interest, fees |
| 5000s | Money we spend |

**Three jobs to do here, once.**

**a) One account per real bank account.** If the society banks with two banks, make an account for each — not one lump called "Bank". You cannot reconcile a lump against two statements.

If you split them, make the parent a heading and put the real accounts underneath:

```
1000  Cash & Bank        (heading — no money sits here)
  1010  Cash in Hand
  1020  FCMB Bank
  1030  UBA Bank
```

**b) Tick "Money moves here".** On the Chart of Accounts there is a column called **Money moves here**. Tick it for every account money actually passes through — each bank, cash in hand, and any control account.

A **control account** is a pot money sits in briefly on its way somewhere else. The commonest one: the employer deducts savings from salaries this month but does not send the money to the society until next month. That money is real and it is ours, but it is not in our bank yet. It sits in a control account until it arrives.

Only the accounts you tick are offered when an officer records money in or out. Tick too few and the officer cannot record the truth. A heading with accounts under it cannot be ticked — the money sits in the ones underneath.

**c) Set the default.** Settings lets you choose the account used when nobody picks one. Choose the account most money actually passes through.

# Part 3 — Members

## 12. Adding one member

**Members → Add Member**

The three that matter: **first name, last name, email**. The email is how they are invited in and how they get statements. A wrong email means a member who never gets started.

Then, when you have it: phone, address, date joined, monthly savings, bank details, next of kin, emergency contact.

Every member gets a **member number** — the society's name for them. Use it on every list and every upload.

## 13. Adding many members at once

**Members → Bulk Upload**

1. **Download the template.** Do not build your own — the columns must match.
2. Fill one row per member.
3. Upload it.
4. Read what comes back. It tells you how many went in, how many were skipped, and exactly which row failed and why.

**Read the errors.** A silent row is a member who does not exist.

## 14. Inviting members in

A member cannot log in until they have set their password.

- One member: open them and send the setup link.
- Everybody at once: **Bulk setup links**, which invites everyone who has not yet set up.

A member's profile shows a **readiness** figure. It reaches 100% when their personal, contact, bank, emergency and next-of-kin details are all complete. Chase the low ones — it is these details you will need on the day somebody dies or a loan goes wrong.

## 15. Editing, leaving, coming back

- **Edit** — open the member, change, save.
- **Mark as former** — when somebody leaves. Their record and history stay. Use this, never delete.
- **Reinstate** — when they come back.

Do not delete a member who has ever saved or borrowed. Their history is part of the society's accounts.

## 16. Member cards

**Members → open a member → Print Card.** A printable membership card with their photo, number and the society's logo.

## 17. What the member sees

Worth knowing, because members will ask you.

On their own phone or computer a member can:

- See their savings balance and every payment
- See their loans, what is left, and when the next payment is due
- Print their own statement
- Apply for a loan
- Update their own address, phone, bank and next of kin
- Ask to change their monthly savings amount
- Transfer money to their own account number, and say what it is for
- Turn off text messages but keep alerts in the app

**When a member says "my savings are wrong", first open their statement with them.** Most disputes end there.

# Part 4 — Money coming in

## 18. Recording one savings payment

**Members → open the member → Record Savings**

1. Amount.
2. Month it is for.
3. Type — monthly, or voluntary.
4. How they paid — cash, transfer, salary deduction.
5. **Which account it went into.** Pick the real one. If it was paid into UBA, choose UBA.
6. Save.

**What the system wrote.** The society's bank has more money, and the society now owes the member more. If your society uses share capital, part of it went to their share instead of their savings, and the receipt says so.

**Why step 5 matters more than it looks.** This is the one field that decides whether your bank reconciliation works at the end of the month. Choose the wrong account and two banks are wrong at once — the one that did not get the money, and the one that did.

## 19. The monthly upload

Most societies collect through the payroll. One file, everybody at once.

**Savings → Salary Upload**

1. Choose the **month**.
2. Check the **batch reference**, or accept the one offered. This is the name of this upload; you will need it if you have to cancel.
3. Choose the **receiving account**. If the employer has deducted but not yet sent the money, choose your control account — not a bank. The money is not at the bank yet.
4. Choose the file. Download the template first if you do not have last month's.
5. Upload, and **read the result**. It says how many went in, how many were skipped, and which rows failed.

A row can name its own account, in a `bank_account` column, if one member's money went somewhere different.

**When the employer sends the money**, record that separately in Accounting → Journal Entry: the bank has it now, and the control account is empty. See §32.

## 20. Cancelling a whole upload

The wrong file. The wrong month. It happens.

**Savings → open the batch → Reverse this upload**

1. Type a **reason**. It is required, and it stays on the record for ever.
2. Confirm.

Every row is cancelled with an opposite entry, members' balances go back to what they were, and the accounts are put right. Nothing is deleted — you can see the upload, the cancellation and the reason.

Then upload the corrected file. The same receipt numbers are accepted again.

## 21. Correcting one member's savings

One member's figure is wrong and everybody else is fine.

**Members → open the member → Adjust Savings**

1. The amount. **Positive to increase, negative to reduce.**
2. The offset account — where the other side goes. Accumulated Surplus is usual for correcting an old figure.
3. A reason. Required, and it appears on the member's own statement beside the correction.

**Do not try to fix a member's savings with a journal entry.** A journal moves the accounts only. The member's balance comes from their savings records, so a journal changes the books and leaves the member's screen exactly as it was. Officers have lost afternoons to this. Adjust Savings writes both sides together.

## 22. Paying savings out

**Members → open the member → Record Payout**

Required: the amount, the account it was **paid from**, a reason, and a file as evidence — the approval, a signed voucher, or the transfer receipt.

**A payout is blocked while the member still owes on a loan.** That is deliberate. Their savings stand behind their loan.

A full payout can also mark them a former member, if they are leaving.

## 23. Members who transfer money themselves

If the society uses them, each member can be given their own account number. Money they transfer arrives automatically and the member is already known.

**Savings → Member account numbers** to issue them and to see what has arrived.

When money arrives that the system cannot match, it waits for you. Open it, say who it belongs to and what it is for — savings, a loan repayment, or a Target Advance — and apply it.

## 24. When a member wants to change their monthly amount

Members can ask through their own screen. The request appears under **Members → Savings requests**. Approve or decline. Either way the member is told.

# Part 5 — Loans

## 25. How a loan travels

A loan passes through hands in a fixed order, and it cannot skip a step.

```
Member applies
      ↓
Guarantors agree          — the members standing for them say yes
      ↓
Secretary                 — checks the papers
      ↓
Treasurer                 — checks the money
      ↓
President                 — final approval, and the money goes
```

Each officer sees only their own step. **Loans → open the loan** shows where it is and who is holding it up.

An application can be raised by the member on their own screen, or by an officer at the office on their behalf (**Loans → Apply**).

**Nobody may approve their own loan**, whatever office they hold. The system refuses it.

## 26. The checks before the money goes

**Loans → open the loan → Due diligence**

For a member on the payroll: confirmation from the employer that the salary carries the repayment, and that the repayment is secured.

For everybody else: bank statement received, credit check done, and repayment secured.

**The last approval is blocked until every check is ticked.** This is not the system being difficult. These checks are what stands between the society and a loan nobody can collect.

## 27. Approving, and choosing the bank

At each step: open the loan, add a comment, **Approve** or **Reject**. A rejection needs a reason, and the member is told.

At the **last** step — the President's — one more field appears: **Disburse From**.

Pick the account the money is actually leaving. A society with two banks does not pay every loan from the same one, and choosing the wrong one puts both banks out.

When the President approves:

- The loan becomes active
- The insurance and the application fee are taken off
- The member is paid the rest
- The accounts are written
- The member is told

For a ₦500,000 loan at 1% each: ₦5,000 insurance held for the insurer, ₦5,000 fee earned by the society, ₦490,000 to the member, and ₦500,000 recorded as owed to us.

## 28. Recording repayments

**One repayment** — Loans → open the loan → Record Repayment. Amount, method, and **which account received it**.

**Many at once** — Loans → Bulk Repayments. Download the template, fill it, choose the receiving account, upload. A row may name its own account.

**If someone pays more than they owe**, the system settles the loan in full and closes it rather than taking the extra.

Each repayment is split into the part that reduces what they owe and the part that is our interest. The system does the splitting.

## 29. Watching the loan book

**Loans** shows every loan, what is left, and which are behind.

Behind means expected repayments have not arrived, aged so you can see how far behind. Take the list to the meeting. It is the honest picture.

# Part 6 — The accounts

*This part is for every officer, not only the Treasurer. You are asked to approve things. You should be able to read what you are approving.*

## 30. Big words, small meanings

| Word | What it actually means |
| --- | --- |
| **Entry** | One recorded event. "We received ₦10,000 from Musa." |
| **Debit** and **credit** | The two sides of every entry. Every entry has both, and they are always equal. That is the whole trick: if they are equal, the books balance. |
| **Account** | A pot. UBA Bank is a pot. Members' savings is a pot. |
| **Ledger** | The full history of one pot. |
| **Balance** | What is in a pot right now. |
| **Trial balance** | All the pots on one page, proving the two sides still equal. |
| **Reversal** | An opposite entry that cancels a wrong one. We never rub out. |
| **Control account** | A pot money sits in briefly on its way somewhere else. |

**You do not need to know which side is which.** The system decides. You need to be able to open an entry and see that it says what you expect.

## 31. What the button wrote

Every button writes an entry. Here is what the common ones write.

**A member saves ₦10,000**
- Our bank has ₦10,000 more
- We owe the member ₦10,000 more

Both sides ₦10,000. Nothing was earned — the money is not ours, we are holding it.

**A ₦500,000 loan is paid out**
- The member owes us ₦500,000
- Our bank has ₦490,000 less
- We earned ₦5,000 in fees
- We hold ₦5,000 for the insurer

**A repayment of ₦53,750 arrives**
- Our bank has ₦53,750 more
- The member owes ₦48,750 less
- We earned ₦5,000 of interest

**To see this for yourself:** record something on the practice copy, then Accounting → Journal Register, and open the newest entry. Everything you did is there, with your name on it.

## 32. Writing your own entry

Most days you never do this. The buttons write the accounts for you. But some events have no button — the employer sending over last month's deductions, a bank charge, correcting an old figure.

**Accounting → Journal Entry**

1. Date.
2. A description a stranger could understand in a year. Not "adjustment". "Employer remittance for September deductions".
3. The lines. Each names an account and an amount on one side.
4. The two sides must be equal, or it will not save.

**Worked example — the employer sends the money.** In September the payroll deducted ₦1,765,000 and it went to your control account. In October the money lands in UBA:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UBA Bank | 1,765,000 | |
| Cooperative Fund Account | | 1,765,000 |

The bank has it now. The control account is empty. Nothing was earned — the money simply moved.

## 33. Finding any entry

Four ways. Learn all four.

**By what happened** — **Accounting → Journal Register.** Every entry, newest first. Search and filter by date.

**By pot** — **Accounting → Chart of Accounts → click a code.** Every entry that touched that account, with a running balance. This is the one to use for "what went through UBA in September".

**By member** — open the member. Their savings statement and loans show their own history, and each line traces to the entry behind it.

**By bank** — **Accounting → Bank Accounts → click an account.** Money in, money out, and the closing balance for any period.

Every one of these has an **Export** button.

## 34. Fixing a mistake

**The rule: never delete. Reverse.**

1. Find the entry (§33).
2. Open it.
3. **Reverse**.
4. Type a reason. Required.

The system writes an opposite entry. The original stays, marked reversed, with your reason. Anyone can see the mistake, the correction and why.

This protects the society, and it protects you. An entry that vanishes looks like something hidden. An entry reversed with a reason looks like an officer doing their job.

**If the reverse button refuses**, the entry came from somewhere with its own way of undoing — a savings upload is cancelled from the batch page, a loan from the loan record. The message tells you where to go.

**A closed month cannot be changed.** Make the correction in the open month, dated today, and explain in the description. See §37.

## 35. Starting balances

*Only when moving in from an old system. Once, at the beginning, and the President and Treasurer do it together.*

You are telling the system what the society owned and owed on the day you started.

1. Agree the date you are moving from. Everything before it is history; everything after is recorded normally.
2. **Data Migration → Opening Balances → download the template.** It lists every account with its code.
3. Fill in what each pot held on that date, from your old trial balance.
4. Upload it, dated the changeover date.

Then bring across the members' own figures — **Data Migration → Import Savings** and **Import Loans** — so that each member's balance adds up to the totals you just posted.

**Three warnings, learned the hard way.**

- **Every account code must exist first.** Add any missing accounts before you upload, or the rows are rejected.
- **Uploading again replaces the previous opening entry.** It does not add to it. So a corrected file is safe to re-upload.
- **Check the members add up to the total.** The savings you import should equal what the opening balance says you owe members. If they differ, find out why before anybody sees a balance.

# Part 7 — Checking the money

## 36. The six end-of-month checks

Do these in order, every month. The Treasurer works, the President watches — because it is the President who tells the AGM the figures are right.

**Check 1 — The bank accounts screen**

**Accounting → Bank Accounts.** Each account, its opening balance, money in, money out, closing balance. Does each look like the month you just lived through?

**Check 2 — Our record against the bank statement**

Open each account and enter the closing balance from the real bank statement. The system shows the difference.

Nil is what you want. If not, the usual reasons:

- A payment recorded here that has not yet cleared the bank
- Bank charges the bank took and we have not recorded — record them (§32)
- Something recorded against the wrong bank — reverse it and record it again (§34)

**Do not carry an unexplained difference into next month.** It never gets smaller.

**Check 3 — Members' savings against what we say we owe**

**Reports → Member Savings Control.** It compares the total of every member's savings against the accounts' figure for what we owe members.

They must be equal. If not, something was recorded on one side and not the other. Settings → System → **Reconcile savings** fixes the commonest cause.

**Check 4 — The loan list**

**Reports → Loan Portfolio.** Every loan, what is left, who is behind. Does the total owed match the accounts? Take the arrears list to the meeting.

**Check 5 — The trial balance and the statements**

**Accounting → Trial Balance.** Two columns, and they must be equal. Then **Reports → Financial** for the income and expenditure and the balance sheet.

Read them. Does the surplus look like the month you had? A number that surprises you is a number to chase.

**Check 6 — Close the month**

Only when the first five are clean. See §37.

## 37. Closing the month

**Accounting → Period Close.** Set the date the books are locked through.

Nothing dated on or before that date can be added or changed. It fixes the month you have just certified.

**If a mistake in a closed month turns up later**, you do not reopen. You correct it in the open month, dated today, describing what it relates to. Everybody can then see both the original month as certified and the correction — which is exactly what an auditor wants.

Only the President should hold this.

# Part 8 — Other money

Four small screens, all the same shape: a list, an **Add** button, a template and an export.

- **Expenses** — what the society spends. Rent, stationery, bank charges, transport.
- **Revenue** — money earned that is not loan interest or fees.
- **Honorarium** — payments to officers. Approved at a meeting before they are recorded.
- **Investments** — money placed to earn a return, and what it earns.

Each writes its own accounting entry, so each shows up in the trial balance and the statements without you doing anything more.

# Part 9 — Sharing the profit

## 39. Dividends

At year end the society shares out its surplus.

**Three rules, in order.**

1. **Check before you compute.** Do the six checks (§36) and close the year first. A dividend calculated on figures that do not agree is a dividend calculated wrongly.
2. **The decision is made outside the system.** The committee or the AGM decides the rate, and it goes in the minutes. The system shares it out; it does not decide it.
3. **Correct a mistake with a fresh entry, never by editing what was declared.** A declared dividend is a promise to members.

**Accounting → Dividends → Declare.** Choose the period and the rate. The system works out each member's share from what they had in, and shows you the whole list before anything is posted.

Read the list. Check a member you know by heart. Then post it.

# Part 10 — The life of the society

## 40. Meetings, minutes and attendance

**Governance**

- **Events** — meetings and society events, with a calendar. Members can say whether they are coming.
- **Attendance** — record who came. It matters for a quorum, and at the AGM.
- **Minutes** — upload the signed minutes. They are kept with the meeting, and officers can find them years later.

The Secretary's screen, mostly.

## 41. Messages to members

**Communications**

Send an email or a text to everybody, or to a group. Reminders about savings, notices of a meeting, a message about the AGM.

Everything sent is kept, with who sent it and who received it.

**Text messages cost money per message. Members using the phone app get alerts free.** For a long notice to everybody, email first.

## 42. Target Advance

*Only if the society runs this scheme. If you do not, skip it.*

Everybody in a group contributes the same amount each period, and a ballot decides who collects when.

**Accounting → CTAS.** It is switched off until the President switches it on.

The parts: **plans** (the shape of a cycle), **cycles** (a running group), **subscriptions** (members who joined), the **ballot** (who collects when), and **exceptions** (somebody who stopped paying).

Three separate checks before somebody joins: are they eligible, can they afford it, and who stands for them. Different officers do different ones, on purpose.

If your society runs this scheme, ask for the separate session on it. It is too big for a paragraph.

# Part 12 — When something is wrong

## 44. Common problems

**"I recorded savings but the member's balance did not change."**
You probably wrote a journal entry. A journal moves the accounts only. Use Adjust Savings (§21).

**"The bank does not agree with the statement."**
Work check 2 in §36. Usually an unrecorded bank charge, or something recorded against the wrong bank.

**"I cannot approve this loan."**
Either a due diligence check is not ticked (§26), or it is not your step, or it is your own loan.

**"I uploaded the wrong file."**
Cancel the whole batch with a reason, then upload the right one (§20).

**"It says I am not allowed."**
Your office does not hold that duty. The President can change it in Task Assignment (§9).

**"I cannot change an entry from three months ago."**
That month is closed. Correct it in the open month, dated today (§37).

**"The account I want is not in the list."**
It has not been ticked as one money moves through. Chart of Accounts → tick **Money moves here** (§11).

**"A member says they never got their statement."**
Check the email on their record. Then Settings → Mail → Send test.

## 45. The rules that keep us safe

Nine rules. If you remember nothing else, remember these.

1. **One login per person. Never share.**
2. **Turn on the six-digit code, and keep the backup codes at home.**
3. **Never delete. Reverse, with a reason.**
4. **Always pick the account the money really moved through.**
5. **Read what an upload tells you afterwards.** A skipped row is a member who was missed.
6. **Do the six checks every month, in order, before closing.**
7. **Do not carry an unexplained difference into next month.**
8. **Nobody approves their own loan, or their own payout.**
9. **When a member disputes a figure, open their statement with them.**
